SECTION 1: SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTING (SAR)
==============================================================================

1.1 Reporting Thresholds

Financial institutions are required under 31 CFR § 1020.320 to file a
Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) when:

  • A transaction involves $5,000 or more in funds or other assets AND
  • The institution knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that the
    transaction involves funds from illegal activity, is designed to evade
    reporting requirements, lacks a lawful purpose, or involves use of the
    institution to facilitate criminal activity.

For wire transfers specifically, institutions must also comply with the
FinCEN Travel Rule (31 CFR § 1010.410(f)), which requires transmitting
institutions to pass along originator and beneficiary information for
transfers of $3,000 or more.

1.2 Filing Deadlines

SARs must be filed within 30 calendar days of the date of the initial
detection of facts that may constitute a basis for filing. If no suspect is
identified, the institution may delay filing for an additional 30 days.
In no case shall reporting be delayed more than 60 days.

SARs are filed electronically via the FinCEN BSA E-Filing System
(bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov).

1.3 Confidentiality

No institution, director, officer, employee, or agent of a financial
institution that files a SAR may notify any person involved in the transaction
that the SAR has been reported. Violators may be subject to civil and
criminal penalties.

SECTION 2: CURRENCY TRANSACTION REPORTS (CTR)
==============================================================================

2.1 Threshold and Requirement

A Currency Transaction Report must be filed for each transaction in currency
(cash) that exceeds $10,000, or for multiple transactions that together exceed
$10,000 if the institution has knowledge that they are by or on behalf of the
same person and result in either cash-in or cash-out totaling more than $10,000
during any one business day.

CTRs must be filed within 15 calendar days of the transaction.

2.2 Exemptions

Certain customers may be designated as exempt from CTR reporting under
31 CFR § 1020.315, including Phase I exempt persons (banks, government
agencies, listed public companies) and Phase II exempt persons (non-listed
businesses and payroll customers with established transaction history).

SECTION 3: KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER (KYC) AND CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE
==============================================================================

3.1 Customer Identification Program (CIP)

Under 31 CFR § 1020.220, the Bank must collect the following minimum
information for all new individual account holders:

  • Full legal name
  • Date of birth
  • Address (residential street address required; P.O. Box insufficient)
  • Identification number (SSN for U.S. persons; passport/TIN for non-U.S.)

For legal entities (corporations, LLCs, partnerships), the Bank must also
collect information on beneficial owners who own 25% or more of the entity
and one individual with significant managerial control.

3.2 Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

EDD is required for:
  • Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) and their immediate family members
  • Customers from FATF high-risk or non-cooperative jurisdictions
  • Correspondent banking relationships
  • Private banking clients with assets exceeding $1 million
  • Any customer presenting elevated risk indicators during onboarding

EDD must include source of wealth and source of funds documentation.

3.3 Ongoing Monitoring

Customer risk profiles must be reviewed:
  • Annually for high-risk customers
  • Every two years for medium-risk customers
  • Every three years for low-risk customers

Triggers for out-of-cycle review include material changes in transaction
patterns, adverse media hits, or changes in ownership/control.

==============================================================================
SECTION 4: OFAC SCREENING
==============================================================================

4.1 Screening Requirements

All new customers and counterparties must be screened against the Office of
Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked
Persons (SDN) List prior to account opening or transaction processing.

Ongoing screening must occur:
  • At each significant transaction (wires, ACH batches over $25,000)
  • When the SDN List is updated (typically within one business day)
  • Annually for the existing customer base

4.2 Match Handling

Any potential OFAC match must be escalated immediately to the BSA Officer.
The transaction must be blocked or rejected pending review. A blocking or
rejection report must be filed with OFAC within 10 business days.

==============================================================================
SECTION 5: RECORD RETENTION
==============================================================================

5.1 Retention Schedule

  • SAR filings and supporting documentation: 5 years from filing date
  • CTR filings: 5 years from filing date
  • Wire transfer records (Travel Rule): 5 years from execution date
  • CIP/KYC documentation: 5 years after account closure
  • OFAC blocking/rejection reports: 5 years from date of report

All records must be retrievable within a reasonable time period and must be
made available to FinCEN, federal banking regulators, and law enforcement
upon request.

==============================================================================
SECTION 6: TRAINING
==============================================================================

All Bank employees must complete BSA/AML training annually. Employees in
customer-facing roles, wire operations, and the BSA/compliance function must
complete role-specific training covering current red flags, typologies, and
regulatory updates. Training completion is tracked in the Learning Management
System (LMS) and is subject to audit.
